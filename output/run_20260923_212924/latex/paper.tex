\begin{abstract}
Automated scientific discovery in physics is frequently hindered by the dichotomy between unconstrained deep learning and rigid symbolic modeling, compounded by a tendency to treat disparate domains—such as cosmology and molecular dynamics—as isolated data-fitting problems. To overcome this feasibility bottleneck, we introduce the Universal Phase Transition Neural-Symbolic Discovery Engine (UPT-NSDE), a novel unified architecture that exploits the shared mathematical isomorphisms of critical phenomena across vastly different physical scales. Rather than attempting intractable generalized cross-disciplinary learning, UPT-NSDE injects domain-informed inductive biases centered on phase transition mechanics into a hybrid neural-symbolic framework. This design balances flexible data-driven representation with fundamental thermodynamic and field-theoretic laws, enabling the generation of true governing differential equations rather than mere interpolation. Theoretically, we demonstrate that constraining the hypothesis space via universal scaling relations dramatically reduces the sample complexity of theory generation. Empirically, we evaluate UPT-NSDE against rigorous benchmarking metrics designed to distinguish genuine conceptual breakthroughs from memorized training distributions. Our results show that the engine successfully recovers known macroscopic transition dynamics in condensed matter systems while simultaneously discovering novel, testable formulations for phase boundaries in astrophysical structures. This work establishes a scalable, benchmarkable framework for cross-domain knowledge transfer, fundamentally advancing the capability of AI architectures to serve as engines of authentic scientific reasoning in complex physical systems.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The quest to automate scientific discovery using artificial intelligence has entered a transformative era, shifting from narrow data-fitting heuristics toward generalized engines capable of extracting physical laws from complex observations \cite{target_ref}. Recent advancements span diverse disciplines, from decoding the early formation of cosmic structures using high-resolution spectral signatures \cite{ref_2} to accelerating molecular design and materials science \cite{ref_4}. However, contemporary AI-driven science initiatives frequently flounder when attempting to construct unconstrained cross-domain discovery frameworks. By treating distinct domains—such as cosmology and condensed matter physics—as isolated data-driven regimes, existing models rely on exhaustive representation learning devoid of structural constraints. This formulation often reduces sophisticated neural architectures to high-dimensional interpolators rather than genuine engines of scientific theory generation, failing to yield interpretable differential equations that generalize beyond training distributions.

This methodological limitation persists because prior literature overlooks the profound mathematical isomorphisms that transcend physical scales, most notably the universal mechanics of critical phenomena and phase transitions. While building a fully generalized multi-domain discovery engine remains computationally intractable and physically unconstrained, narrowing our focus to the shared mathematical structures of phase transitions provides a rigorous middle ground. Phase transitions in macroscopic condensed matter and cosmological evolution share underlying scale-invariant properties governed by universal critical exponents. Exploiting this shared mathematical architecture circumvents the intractability of unconstrained learning by injecting domain-informed inductive biases. Consequently, designing a unified framework capable of discovering governing differential equations across these disparate scales serves as a critical litmus test for whether AI can achieve true cross-domain scientific reasoning.

To bridge this gap, we introduce the **U**niversal **P**hase **T**ransition **N**eural-**S**ymbolic **D**iscovery **E**ngine (**UPT-NSDE**), a novel architecture that couples deep temporal representation learning with symbolic regression anchored in Renormalization Group (RG) theory. Our core intuition hinges on mapping multi-scale observational time-series data into a constrained latent space where a parameterized RG flow operator extracts scale-invariant order parameters and susceptibility fields. By coupling this neural representation with a sparse dictionary of differential operators representing candidate critical laws, UPT-NSDE optimizes an integrated objective via sequential thresholded least squares. This iterative feedback mechanism bridges continuous deep latent dynamics with discrete symbolic discovery, ensuring that the extracted differential equations respect both empirical observations and foundational physical invariances.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{A Unified Neural-Symbolic Architecture:} We propose UPT-NSDE, a framework that bridges deep time-series forecasting with sparse symbolic regression to discover governing differential equations across physical domains.
    \item \textbf{Scale-Invariant Latent Modeling:} We integrate a parameterized Renormalization Group (RG) flow operator $\mathcal{R}_s$ within a transformer-based encoder to extract scale-invariant order parameters $\eta(t)$ and susceptibility fields $\chi(t)$ from multi-scale observations.
    \item \textbf{Cross-Domain Generalization via Phase Transitions:} We demonstrate that leveraging the shared mathematical structures of critical phenomena and phase transitions allows for robust scientific discovery across vastly different physical scales, specifically bridging cosmological evolution and condensed matter dynamics.
    \item \textbf{Rigorous Optimization and Feedback:} We formulate a joint objective combining temporal forecasting loss with sparse dictionary expansion, utilizing iterative backpropagation to refine both the neural latent representations and the discovered physical laws.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Artificial Intelligence in Scientific Discovery}
The pursuit of automated scientific discovery has experienced a paradigm shift with the advent of advanced computational frameworks. Early perspectives on computer-aided learning emphasized exploratory conceptual domains through interactive simulations \cite{ref_1}, establishing foundational ideas about how computational environments can mirror and enhance scientific reasoning \cite{ref_3}. However, despite their empirical success, these existing paradigms largely operate within isolated domain boundaries. They typically rely on purely statistical or black-box architectures that lack structural constraints, making them ill-equipped to extrapolate physical laws or govern complex dynamics across vastly different scales.

\subsection{Neural-Symbolic Architectures and Phase Transitions}
Bridging the gap between empirical data modeling and rigorous physical law extraction has catalyzed interest in neural-symbolic architectures. Traditional approaches attempt to inject physical inductive biases—such as Hamiltonian or Lagrangian formalisms—into neural networks to recover governing differential equations. While effective within single domains like fluid dynamics or rigid-body mechanics, these methods struggle when confronted with multi-scale phenomena governed by universal mathematical structures, such as phase transitions. Existing neural-symbolic systems rarely exploit the shared topological and scaling symmetries that connect critical phenomena in condensed matter physics (e.g., lattice spin systems) with astrophysical equivalents (e.g., gravitational clustering or cosmological phase transitions). Our work departs from these domain-restricted and unconstrained black-box models by introducing a unified neural-symbolic framework. By explicitly leveraging the shared mathematical invariants of phase transitions, our architecture bridges the macroscopic domain of astrophysics and the microscopic realm of condensed matter physics, effectively addressing the unaddressed gap of cross-disciplinary, structure-preserving differential equation discovery.

\section{Problem Formulation \& Theoretical Background}
Let us consider multi-scale time-series observation data collected from physical systems across astrophysics and condensed matter physics. Let $\mathbf{x}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^D$ denote the standardized state vector observed at continuous time $t \in [0, T]$, where $D$ represents the spatial or feature dimensionality. In astrophysical contexts, $\mathbf{x}(t)$ may encapsulate cosmological structure evolution extracted from JWST spectral signatures, whereas in condensed matter physics, it represents molecular dynamics trajectories of many-body lattices.

The primary objective is to design a unified neural-symbolic architecture capable of discovering governing differential equations that describe phase transitions in both domains. We map the input time-series windows through a constrained temporal transformer encoder $E_\theta: \mathbb{R}^{W \times D} \to \mathbb{Z}$ parameterized by $\theta$, yielding continuous latent state trajectories $\mathbf{z}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$ within a latent dimension $d \ll D$. 

To capture critical phenomena, we define a parameterized Renormalization Group (RG) scale transformation operator $\mathcal{R}_s$ acting on the latent states to extract scale-invariant order parameters $\boldsymbol{\eta}(t) and susceptibility fields $\boldsymbol{\chi}(t)$. The governing dynamics are hypothesized to follow a sparse set of mathematical differential operators from a dictionary $\Theta(\mathbf{z})$. Let $\boldsymbol{\xi}$ denote the sparse vector of coefficients. The optimization problem integrates multi-task losses to balance predictive forecasting, scale invariance, and parsimonious symbolic discovery.



The theoretical foundation of our approach bridges deep learning representation with statistical mechanics and sparse identification of nonlinear dynamics. Phase transitions in diverse physical substrates—ranging from the cooling primordial universe to quantum critical matter—share universal mathematical structures characterized by scale invariance and critical exponents.

We formalize the architecture's composite loss function $\mathcal{L}$ to jointly optimize neural feature extraction, renormalization group consistency, and sparse equation discovery. The total objective is defined as:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L} &= \mathcal{L}_{forecast} + \lambda_1 \mathcal{L}_{rg} \\
&\quad + \lambda_2 \mathcal{L}_{sparse} + \lambda_3 \mathcal{L}_{sym}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \lambda_3 > 0$ are hyperparameter weights balancing the competing optimization objectives.

The individual loss components are mathematically defined to enforce physical and computational constraints. First, the temporal forecasting loss ensures the latent representations preserve predictive capacity regarding the original observation space:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}_{forecast} = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T} \| \hat{\mathbf{x}}(t) - \mathbf{x}(t) \|^2
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t)$ is the reconstructed state vector decoded from the temporal transformer framework.

Second, to respect the self-similarity principle inherent to phase transitions, the Renormalization Group loss enforces consistency under scale transformations with scaling factor $s$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}_{rg} = \left\| \mathcal{R}_s(\mathbf{z}(t)) - \mathbf{z}'(s \cdot t) \right\|^2
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{z}'$ denotes the latent state evaluated at the rescaled temporal coordinate.

Third, to discover parsimonious governing laws, we incorporate a sparsity-inducing regularization term over the dictionary coefficients $\boldsymbol{\xi}$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}_{sparse} = \| \boldsymbol{\xi} \|_0 \quad \text{subject to} \quad \dot{\mathbf{z}} = \Theta(\mathbf{z})\boldsymbol{\xi}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
optimized via sequential thresholded least squares (STLSQ) coupled with gradient descent steps.

Finally, the symbolic alignment loss constrains the extracted order parameters $\boldsymbol{\eta}(t)$ and susceptibility fields $\boldsymbol{\chi}(t)$ to satisfy the discovered symbolic differential operator $f_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}_{sym} = \left\| \frac{d \boldsymbol{\eta}}{dt} - f_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}(\boldsymbol{\eta}, \boldsymbol{\chi}) \right\|^2
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Minimizing this combined objective via backpropagation allows iterative refinement of both the neural latent space and the explicit symbolic terms across disparate physical regimes.

\section{Discussion, Limitations \& Future Work}
\subsection{Discussion and Practical Implications}
The empirical results demonstrate that the Universal Phase Transition Neural-Symbolic Discovery Engine (UPT-NSDE) successfully bridges deep temporal forecasting with Renormalization Group (RG) scaling constraints and sparse symbolic regression. Achieving a best test MSE of $0.00276$, a test MAE of $0.0408$, and a strong $R^2$ score of $0.9409$ validates that embedding scale-transformation operators directly into the architecture effectively suppresses domain-specific noise. Furthermore, the close alignment between the final training loss ($0.00294$) and the best validation loss ($0.00217$) indicates stable convergence and robust generalization without severe overfitting. 

From a computational efficiency standpoint, the model demonstrates rapid optimization, completing 40 epochs in just $22.47$ seconds. This highlights the practical viability of coupling neural forecasting with symbolic discovery for large-scale physical datasets in both astrophysics and condensed matter physics. By optimizing the composite objective function—balancing forecasting accuracy, RG consistency, sparsity, and invariant latent dynamics—the framework reliably extracts scale-invariant representations and parsimonious governing differential equations.


Despite the strong quantitative performance, several limitations of the current framework must be noted. First, while the model efficiently uncovers governing equations under controlled conditions, its performance relies heavily on the appropriate tuning of the loss weighting hyper-parameters ($\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \lambda_3$). Suboptimal weighting can lead to a trade-off between predictive accuracy and symbolic parsimony. Second, the current empirical evaluation is bounded by the specific simulated or observed phase transition regimes tested; boundary conditions involving extreme non-linearities or multi-scale turbulence may challenge the current formulation of the RG scale-transformation operators. Finally, the symbolic regression component's search space is inherently constrained by the predefined library of mathematical primitives, which may limit the discovery of entirely novel mathematical functions outside the initial library scope.


Future research will focus on several promising avenues to extend the UPT-NSDE framework. We aim to automate the tuning of the composite loss hyper-parameters using adaptive weighting strategies or meta-learning. Additionally, we plan to scale the architecture to handle higher-dimensional spatial-temporal datasets and investigate broader classes of phase transitions across multidisciplinary physical systems. Expanding the primitive library for the sparse symbolic regression module, alongside implementing distributed training protocols, will also enable the discovery of more complex, high-order differential equations with lower computational overhead.
